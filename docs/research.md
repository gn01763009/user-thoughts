# Research behind the design

A condensed record of what was read before writing this skill, in November 2026. Most sources were read at abstract or summary level, and some numbers come from secondary coverage. Check the original before citing anything here.

## Does this already exist?

Not as a whole. Several tools run personas against live sites and report friction or abandonment, and those are listed in the [README](../README.md#related-projects). None of those surveyed treats "should this feature exist?" as its main output. Commercial products such as Loop11 AI Browser Agents and Uxia claim similar things, but the evidence found was vendor material.

## How far simulated users can be trusted

The independent evidence is mostly negative.

- [Kuric et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18302), 3,431 participants across 12 first-click tests: GPT click distributions differed significantly on 53% of tasks. Adjusting persona detail and reasoning settings made output look more credible without making it more realistic.
- [Nielsen Norman Group (2024)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/): synthetic users claimed to finish every course and praised a forum real users disliked. Sample sizes were not published. Conclusion: use them for preparation and hypotheses, not in place of people.
- [Seshadri et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.17087): simulated users miscalibrated agent success rates, and differently across user groups.
- [UXAgent (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09407): the closest research system. Its authors say it is not meant to replace participants, and 16 UX researchers rated that prospect below neutral.
- [Userbrain](https://userbrain.com/blog/synthetic-users-experiment/): a small vendor experiment, five per group, with a stated conflict of interest. Unadjusted synthetic users worked through every option like an accountant.
- [Hullman et al. (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.15785): confirmatory work needs calibration against human samples. Exploratory use is the better fit for simulation.
- LLM willingness-to-pay estimates can run low, about 27% in [one study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-026-00542-7).

No study was found that directly tests whether an LLM persona can tell that a feature is not wanted. That is the central assumption of this skill and it is unproven.

Some findings do support structured use:

- [Zhong et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02306): synthetic heuristic evaluation found 73% and 77% of issues, against 57% and 63% for five experts, with weaknesses on cross-screen issues.
- [Platt et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04262): the same model across three runs agreed on issue detection at Cohen's κ 0.50, and exact severity agreement was 56%. This is why severity is computed here rather than assigned by a model.
- Human evaluators disagree too. In [Jacobsen et al. (1998)](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/the-evaluator-effect-in-usability-studies-problem-detection-and-s/), four evaluators watching the same recordings all found only 20% of 93 problems.

## Why builders misjudge users

- [Thompson, Hamilton and Rust (2005)](https://acawiki.org/Feature_Fatigue:_When_Product_Capabilities_Become_Too_Much_of_a_Good_Thing): people overweight capability before use and underweight usability, then regret it.
- [Norton, Mochon and Ariely (2012)](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/7312037d-2473-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/content): the IKEA effect. Applying it to features generated by AI in hours is a reasonable inference, without direct evidence.
- "Most features go unused" should be cited carefully. The 64% figure traces to [four internal projects](https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/blog/are-64-of-features-really-rarely-or-never-used). No primary source was found for 45%. The 80% figure comes from [Pendo's own customers](https://www.pendo.io/resources/the-2019-feature-adoption-report/), and Pendo sells adoption tools.
- Choice overload is not a general rule: [Scheibehenne et al. (2010)](https://abcwest.cogs.indiana.edu/pmwiki/pdf/scheibehenne.options.2010.pdf) found a mean effect near zero across 50 experiments. So the skill looks for duplicate paths and interference, not for "too many options".

On AI features, the firmest evidence is public retreats rather than design criticism: [Apple paused notification summaries for news apps](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/business/money-report/apple-disables-ai-notifications-for-news-in-its-beta-iphone-software/6110884/?amp=1) in January 2025, and [Microsoft reduced Copilot entry points](https://www.itpro.com/software/windows/microsoft-is-rolling-back-unnecessary-copilot-features-on-windows) in March 2026, the latter from secondary reporting. [Cicek et al. (2024)](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/07/240730134844.htm) found that mentioning AI lowered purchase intent, with no independent replication found. Criticism of "AI-looking" design is [opinion](https://muz.li/blog/vibe-design-in-2026-what-ai-generated-ui-means-for-your-work/), not measurement.

Visible manipulation can be checked directly. [deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/) lists the patterns, and an [FTC and ICPEN sweep](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/10/ftc-study-finds-dark-patterns-used-by-a-majority-of-subscription-apps-and-websites) found about 76% of 642 subscription services used at least one.

Looking from outside cannot show usage. So the skill reports what a feature costs a visitor and asks a question, and it does not claim a feature is unwanted.

## User roles

The eight original archetypes combine these sources. The combination is an inference, not a validated taxonomy. `committed-user` was added after testing, for mandated use, where the user is not the buyer.

- [NN/g persona types](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/persona-types/): proto, qualitative and statistical personas. Proto personas can become an echo chamber, which is why each file here states its evidence.
- [NN/g e-commerce shoppers](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ecommerce-shoppers/): product-focused, browsers, researchers, bargain hunters, one-time shoppers.
- Alan Cooper's perpetual intermediates, Steve Krug's scanning and muddling through, and Pirolli and Card's information scent. These were read through secondary summaries.
- [Broder (2002)](https://researchr.org/publication/Broder02): navigational, informational and transactional intent.
- [Microsoft Inclusive Design](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/training/modules/inclusive-design-principles/3-inclusive-design-principles): permanent, temporary and situational limits.
- [W3C WAI Stories of Web Users](https://www.w3.org/WAI/people-use-web/user-stories/).
- [GenderMag](https://gendermag.org/): cognitive facets, used here as behaviour rather than as gender labels.
- [Muller](https://synthetic-data-workshop.github.io/papers/10.pdf): a critique of synthetic personas replacing real people.

Attributes kept are those that change behaviour on a page: goal, entry point, patience, reading mode, trust, device, interruption, assistive technology and literacy. Name, photo and unrelated demographics are decoration. Descriptive models are rewritten as observable rules, because a model treats "impatient" as a personality rather than a limit.

B2B roles come from vendor blogs and are the weakest evidence here. No specific abandonment threshold was found in the literature, so all limits are design assumptions.

## Evaluation methods

- [Cognitive walkthrough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_walkthrough): four questions per step, recorded here as `cw`. Wording varies between sources.
- [NN/g heuristic evaluation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/): three to five independent evaluators, then consolidation.
- Severity scales, Shneiderman's rules, and the five-second, first-click and tree test procedures were not confirmed from primary sources in this pass.

## How popular skills are written

Star counts were read on 2026-11-06: mattpocock/skills 316,067; obra/superpowers 313,299; forrestchang/andrej-karpathy-skills 230,066; anthropics/skills 187,960; garrytan/gstack 140,289. Popularity says little about effectiveness.

Most techniques in these collections, such as iron laws, red-flag lists and rationalisation tables, are rhetoric without measurement. Superpowers cites [persuasion research](https://gail.wharton.upenn.edu/research-and-insights/persuading-llms-initial-study/), but that work measured compliance with objectionable requests on one model, so transfer to following a skill is unproven.

The mechanisms worth borrowing can be checked by a machine:

- Require a quote before a finding keeps high confidence.
- Treat "no findings" as a valid result.
- Keep severity, confidence and evidence separate.
- Dedupe by fingerprint, and let only distinct sources raise confidence.
- Do arithmetic outside the model.
- Use numeric stop conditions.
- Give verifiers the location but not the conclusion.
- Hand off through files, with only a status in the reply.

[skill-creator](https://github.com/anthropics/skills) has the only reproducible evaluation method found, running with and without the skill in the same turn. Its authoring docs also recommend building evaluation scenarios before writing much documentation.

Rules that shaped this repository:

- [Agent Skills specification](https://agentskills.io/specification): `name` must match its folder, `description` up to 1024 characters, keep `SKILL.md` under 500 lines.
- [Claude Code skills](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills): `allowed-tools` is not gated by workspace trust, so this skill omits it.
- [Sub-agents](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents): 20 concurrent by default, and plugin-shipped subagents cannot bring their own MCP servers.
- [Plugin reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugins-reference): plugin names cannot start with `claude-`.
- [Anthropic's multi-agent research post](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system): coordinator delegates, agents write to files, and cost is around 15 times a chat.

Skills do not always trigger. [Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/agents-md-outperforms-skills-in-our-agent-evals) reported the default skill was never invoked in 56% of cases, and [Tessl](https://tessl.io/blog/a-proposed-framework-for-evaluating-skills-research-eng-blog) reported 40.6% activation unforced. Both are vendor reports.

## Safety

- [OWASP LLM01](https://genai.owasp.org/llmrisk/llm01-prompt-injection/): segregate external content, use least privilege, require approval for risky actions, test adversarially.
- [Snyk ToxicSkills](https://snyk.io/blog/toxicskills-malicious-ai-agent-skills-clawhub): 76 confirmed malicious skills among 3,984. This is a vendor report.
- [RFC 9309](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9309.html): `robots.txt` is not access authorisation. The boundaries in the README are this project's decisions, not an industry standard.
